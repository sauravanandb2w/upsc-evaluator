                🎯
                Active Recall
                A · Retrieval Practice
                Don't re-read. Close the book and retrieve.

                Every time you pull information out of memory, you strengthen the neural pathway to it. Re-reading gives an illusion of knowing — recognition is not recall.

                How to use it:
                After reading a section, close it. Write down everything you remember — dates, names, concepts — without peeking. Then check.

                Flash cards Self-testing Blank-paper recall Past papers first
                🔁
                Repeat with Spacing
                R · Spaced Repetition
                Review before you forget — not while you still remember.

                Memory consolidates during sleep and across time. Cramming loads information into short-term memory; spacing moves it into long-term memory.

                Contrast & Chunk
                C · Interference Reduction
                Don't memorise a list. Understand what makes each item different.

                Similar items interfere with each other in memory. Chunking binds related items into one unit. Your working memory holds ~4 chunks (Cowan, 2001) — not 4 raw facts.

                For UPSC: Instead of listing 30 national parks, group by: biome type → then contrast within each. Notice what makes Kaziranga unique vs Manas.

                Comparison tables Mind maps Why is X different? Group by theme
                🔐
                Stick with Encoding Tricks
                S · Dual Coding & Mnemonics
                Give your brain two hooks, not one — words AND images.

                The brain stores verbal and visual information in separate systems. When you encode a concept with both (a word and a mental image or diagram), you double your retrieval routes (Paivio, 1971).

                Encoding toolkit:
                🔤Acronyms — VIBGYOR, HOMES, ARCS
                🖼Visual story — place items on a mental walk (Method of Loci)
                🔗Link to what you know — new info hooks onto existing schema
                🗺Sketch it — even a rough diagram beats bullet points
                Memory palace Draw diagrams Teach aloud

                ⚡ Apply this to UPSC preparation today

                After every mock test, close the answer key and write what you remember about each wrong answer from memory.
                Tag difficult questions in this app (★★★+) to create a personal spaced-repetition list for Day 3, 7, 21.
                When you see two similar topics (e.g. two constitutional amendments), write down: "how are they different?" before moving on.
                Use the Tricks (T1, T2…) feature in this app to attach a mnemonic or image cue to every tricky question.
                Before sleeping, do a 5-minute blank-paper brain dump of everything covered that day — no notes.
                Rotate subjects weekly rather than finishing one topic entirely — interleaving improves long-term retention.
                Sources: Roediger & Karpicke (2006) · Ebbinghaus (1885) · Cowan (2001) · Paivio (1971) · Cepeda et al. (2006)
